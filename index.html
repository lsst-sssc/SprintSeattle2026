Aims and Goals

The Vera C. Rubin Observatory is a new next-generation survey facility on Cerro Pachón, Chile. It houses the 8.4m Simonyi Survey Telescope coupled with the 3.2 Gigapixel LSSTCam camera. The Rubin began it's ten-year survey (the Legacy Survey of Space and Time; LSST) in June 2026. Enabled by its 9.6 square degree field of view and a cadence that will image the sky in multiple filters every 3-4 days to ~24.5 mag, within a year the LSST will become the largest catalog of small body observations to date. This survey will discover millions of new objects ranging from orbits inward of Venus to far beyond that of Neptune. The LSST will go beyond just discovery; over its 10-year mission, it will obtain broad-band optical colors and phase curves, and perform real-time monitoring capturing episodes of cometary activity, changes in orbit, asteroid collisions, rotational breakup events, as well as rotational brightness variations. With its volume, richness, and precision, Rubin Observatory and the LSST dramatically advance the understanding of the Solar System.

The LSST Solar System Science Collaboration (SSSC) is preparing methods and tools to analyze this data and gather people together to dive into this unprecedented dataset. With the release of pre-LSST data and the begining of alert stream now is the perfect time to get the community together to spend 3 dedicated days working with early Rubin science data.
